# Pacific Coast Militia Rangers: Company 71

The displayed PCMR preset reproduces the fixed lettering of the wooden Company 71 plate held by the City of Penticton Archives. The [BCpl8s National Defence article](https://www.bcpl8s.ca/NationalDefence.html) identifies this wooden example separately from the other companies' plates. It is not evidence of a province-wide PCMR font, embossed die, or standard physical dimensions.

Reference: [Company 71 full photograph](https://www.bcpl8s.ca/images/PCMR/PCMR-71(XL).jpg), 2000 × 996 pixels; reviewed October 5, 2026 UTC. The full photograph was checked against an independent full-size reconstruction before the actual application render was integrated.

The previous small-legend construction omitted the most visible features: very heavy narrow P, a curved C with wedge-shaped terminals, deep triangular M notches, a broad diagonal R leg, and four small diamond stops. The replacement constructs those five observed characters as smooth filled outlines. Paint wear, wood grain, chipped edges and individual brush irregularities are excluded. Filled outlines are appropriate here because the painted M and bowl strokes differ from a constant-width stamped centreline.

The right-hand C/O/7/1 column has its own proportions. Its nearly round bowls, broad diagonal 7 and footed 1 should not inherit the large abbreviation's tall narrow C. Only those four observed column characters are supported. Unobserved characters are rejected in both profiles.

The assumed 300 × 150 canvas uses a 76-unit serial height, baseline 111.5 and center 132; the compact column is centered at 267 with baselines 42/72/102/132 and height 24. Letter origins and punctuation use explicit pair placement: P→C→M→R origins are 0/65/134/214 in normalized 100-unit-height coordinates; punctuation sits in the open lower space after each letter. The rim is inset 9 units and widened to 2.6. These measurements describe a normalized visual reconstruction, not certified plate dimensions.

Residual differences include the hand-painted C/R shoulders, slight top/bottom unevenness, photographed perspective and the exact cream paint. A smooth family construction cannot reproduce every brush stroke. Source attribution confirms the specimen and company; it does not establish the plate's legal status or identical tooling for any other PCMR plate.
