# Early British Columbia trailer stamps

BCpl8s [Trailer study](https://www.bcpl8s.ca/Trailer.htm) distinguishes the interlaced monogram of 1921–22 from the upright BC abbreviation introduced in 1923. The T prefix appeared in 1927. The 1948 TR run retained smaller letters beside full-height figures.

The updated presets are:

- `trailer-1921`: monogram restricted to 1921–22; this source reconstruction remains fixed at 1921.
- `trailer-1923`: new unprefixed upright BC/date layout for 1923–26, with 1925 BC323 as the principal reference.
- `trailer-1927`: smaller separate T, 1927–30; documented approximately 180 × 104 mm size.
- `trailer-1931`: new approximately 2:1 longer layout, checked on 1931 T1159, 1933 T164 and 1935 T263. The estimated 205 × 104 mm dimensions and transition date are not certified manufacturing measurements.
- `trailer-1936`: separate smaller T/TR, checked on 1936 T1941, 1937 T2458, 1939 T242, 1940 T772 and 1948 TR510; approximately 205 × 104 mm is documented in the source.

BC uses the same protected upright rounded construction across these early trailer layouts, with a separate small date profile. It does not borrow the small BRITISH COLUMBIA legend from a passenger plate. Numeric figures also use a stable smooth construction after inspection of the early trailer gallery; they are not assigned a slanted passenger die solely because the photograph is dated 1931 or 1936. Visible similarity does not establish that every letter or digit used identical physical tooling.

The smaller prefix is rendered as an independent run (`serial-prefix`), with its own cap height, baseline, position and die. Full-height digits (`serial-number`) keep their own layout even when the prefix changes from T to TR. Export metadata retains the full registration and records both cap heights and prefix profile. The prefix cap is 38.8% of plate height and numeric cap 63.6%, a ratio of approximately 0.61 before fitting. Numbers remain uniformly scaled; no individual occurrence is stretched to fit.

Photographed palettes are provided for 1923, 1924, 1925, 1926, 1927, 1928, 1931, 1935, 1936, 1937, 1939, 1940 and 1948. These colours are approximate readings from aged photographs, with rust explicitly noted where relevant. Other years are not assigned invented palettes.

The comparison page shows six complete original photographs beside actual current SVG exports and an opacity overlay. This is a whole-frame comparison, not a corner-registered homography or proof of precise glyph agreement. Smooth constructions exclude paint spread, wear and embossed shoulders; some numerical contours, dates and spacing remain approximate. The original source links are retained beside every specimen.
